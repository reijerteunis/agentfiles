# Question bank

Good questions are about specifics in the past. Each one below has a weak version to avoid.

| Ask | Instead of |
|---|---|
| "Tell me about the last time you had to <task>. What happened, step by step?" | "Do you find <task> hard?" |
| "How long did that take? Who else was involved?" | "Is it time-consuming?" |
| "What did you do about it? What have you tried?" | "Would a tool help?" |
| "What does that cost you today, in money, hours or risk?" | "Would you pay for a solution?" |
| "What did you buy or build to deal with it? Why did you stop, or keep using it?" | "What features would you want?" |
| "Who decides on tools like that? Whose budget is it?" | "Would your company buy this?" |
| "Where does this rank among your problems this quarter?" | "Is this important to you?" |
| "When did this last cause a real problem: a missed deadline, a fine, an unhappy client?" | "Is this painful?" |
| "Who else deals with this that I should talk to?" | (forgetting to ask) |

## Commitment signals, strongest first

1. Money: a pre-payment, a deposit, a signed pilot fee.
2. A letter of intent, or a signed pilot agreement.
3. Their time on a follow-up: reviewing a prototype, a second session.
4. An introduction to a colleague, their boss, or another customer.
5. A compliment ("great idea!"). That's not a signal.

## Red flags in your own interviews

- You talked more than they did.
- You described your solution before minute 15.
- You heard "I would…" more than "I did…".
- You left without a next step or an introduction.
