# Funding path

Money is a tool, not a milestone. The right path depends on the business model's shape, how much control the founder wants to keep, and how fast the market moves. Many good businesses should never raise.

## 1. Choose the path

| Path | Fits when | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrap or pre-sales** | Customers pay early; the founder wants control; there's a services bridge | Slower growth; the founder's time |
| **Keep the job a while longer** | The idea is still being validated; runway is short | Time and energy; side-project limits |
| **Grants and R&D tax incentives** | Real R&D or innovation, and the paperwork is acceptable | Admin; conditions; usually co-financing |
| **Accelerator or founder programme** | Pre-team or pre-product; needs a network and a structure | Equity (typically a few percent) and time |
| **Angels** | Early traction and a story an operator can back; you need smart money | Small rounds; managing many people |
| **Pre-seed fund** | A venture-scale market, a strong team, and early evidence | Dilution; expectations of venture-scale growth |

Recommend a path with the reason, using `business-model` (the shape) and `profile/founder.md` (the goals). Look up programmes, terms and grant schemes live, because they change. Name examples only as starting points: an accelerator's current terms, the national R&D incentives (for example WBSO in the Netherlands), and EU programmes. Verify the details.

## 2. Prepare

- **The narrative,** one page: the problem with evidence, why now, the solution, traction (the commitments, not the opinions), the market (bottom-up, from `market-map`), why this team, the business model, the ask and what it achieves.
- **An accelerator application:** answer each question specifically and with evidence, and keep it short. Reuse the narrative, but never paste it. Prepare for the interview with the 10 hardest questions and honest answers.
- **An investor list:** funds and angels that actually invest at this stage, in this sector and geography, with the warm-introduction path for each. Cite sources.
- **A data-room starter:** the pitch, the market map, the model, the interview synthesis, the experiment results, the team, cap-table assumptions, and a clean side-project check.

## 3. Outreach

Draft introduction requests and investor emails as drafts only: short, specific, with one line of traction and a clear ask. Prefer warm introductions. Track every conversation with `follow-up-tracker` if it's available.

## Rules

- Never overstate traction. Opinions aren't traction, and LOIs aren't revenue.
- Not investment or legal advice. Term sheets, SAFEs or convertible notes, and shareholder agreements go to a lawyer.
