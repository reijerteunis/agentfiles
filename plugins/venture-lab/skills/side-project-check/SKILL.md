# Side-project check

The most expensive mistake an employed founder can make is building something their employer can claim, or that breaks their contract. Check it before the idea goes public, before money changes hands, and before a company is registered. This is a structured check, not legal advice. Anything that isn't clearly safe goes to an employment lawyer.

## 1. Read the contract and the policies

Ask for the employment contract and the employee handbook or policies, then quote the relevant clauses:

| Clause | Look for |
|---|---|
| IP and inventions | Who owns work "made in the course of employment", and anything "related to the employer's business". Broad clauses that claim everything are a red flag. |
| Side activities | A ban or a consent requirement. Note the jurisdiction rules: some limit how far a ban reaches. |
| Conflict of interest | A duty to disclose anything that could compete with or influence the job |
| Non-compete and non-solicit | Scope, duration and territory, during and after employment |
| Confidentiality | What counts as confidential, and for how long |

## 2. Check the facts that decide ownership

- **Time:** is it built only outside working hours?
- **Equipment and accounts:** personal laptop, personal accounts, personal cloud; never the employer's.
- **Knowledge and data:** no employer code, data, customer lists, roadmaps or confidential insights.
- **Overlap:** does the idea compete with, sell to, or build on the employer's business or customers? Overlap is the biggest risk, especially for senior roles like CTO.
- **People:** no recruiting colleagues while employed (watch for non-solicit clauses), and no employer customers as interviewees or pilot users.

## 3. Jurisdiction

Rules differ by country. Read `references/netherlands.md` for Dutch law, which is dated, and verify that it's still current. For other countries, say that local rules apply and that a lawyer should check them.

## 4. Rate and advise

- **Green:** no overlap, a clean contract, and personal time and equipment only. Proceed with the safe-practice rules.
- **Amber:** a broad IP clause, a consent requirement, adjacent markets, or senior-role sensitivity. Get legal advice, and consider asking the employer for a written carve-out.
- **Red:** competes with the employer, uses their resources, or a clause clearly claims it. Stop or restructure before going further.

## 5. Safe-practice rules, for every amber or green idea

- Personal time, devices, accounts and email only. Keep a dated log of when and where the work was done.
- Nothing from the employer: no code, data, documents, contacts or confidential knowledge.
- Keep it quiet publicly until the check is green and disclosure (if needed) is done. Follow the founder's discretion rules.
- If disclosure or consent is needed, prepare a short, factual, written request describing what the project isn't (not competing, no employer resources), drafted for the founder to send.

## Output

A `side-project-check.md` in the idea folder: the clauses quoted, the facts checklist, the jurisdiction notes, the rating, the safe-practice rules, and the questions for the lawyer.
