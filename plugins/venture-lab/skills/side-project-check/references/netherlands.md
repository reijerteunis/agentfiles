# Netherlands notes

**As of October 2026.** A summary for orientation, not legal advice. Verify against the current law and case law, and ask an employment lawyer (arbeidsrechtadvocaat) for anything that isn't clearly safe.

## Side activities (nevenwerkzaamheden)

- Since 1 August 2022, **article 7:653a of the Dutch Civil Code (BW)** makes a clause that bans or limits work for others outside working hours **void**, unless the employer can justify it on an **objective ground**. It implements the EU Directive on transparent and predictable working conditions.
- The Directive gives examples of objective grounds: health and safety, protecting business confidentiality, the integrity of public services, and **avoiding conflicts of interest**. The list is illustrative, not exhaustive. Courts have upheld restrictions on side activities that **harm or compete** with the employer.
- It also applies to contracts signed before 1 August 2022. The justification doesn't have to be written in the contract; the employer can give it later.
- An employee may not be disadvantaged for invoking this right (article 7:653a paragraph 2).
- A consent requirement is a restriction too: a refusal needs an objective reason tied to the activity and the role.

**What this means in practice:** a blanket ban is probably unenforceable, but a restriction on competing or conflicting activities can hold. Senior roles make conflict-of-interest arguments stronger.

## Intellectual property

- **Copyright:** under article 7 of the Dutch Copyright Act (Auteurswet), when an employee creates a work in the course of their duties, the employer is regarded as its author. Whether a side project falls "within the duties" depends on the job description and on the facts: time, equipment and overlap. Contracts often widen this with IP clauses.
- **Patents:** employee inventions can belong to the employer when the nature of the job involves inventing (Dutch Patent Act, Rijksoctrooiwet 1995). This matters mostly for patentable technology.

## Non-compete (concurrentiebeding)

- It must be in writing, agreed with an adult employee. In a fixed-term contract it's only valid with a written justification of the substantial business interest.
- A court can annul or limit a non-compete, wholly or partly, if the employee's interest outweighs the employer's.
- Check the scope, the duration and the territory, and whether a non-solicitation (relatiebeding) clause applies as well.
- **Coming, not in force:** a bill to modernise the non-compete went to the Council of State on 29 June 2026, aiming for parliament by the end of 2026. It would cap a non-compete at one year, require the employer to pay compensation when invoking it, and require the area to be stated ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/06/26/meer-vrijheid-voor-werknemer-door-modernisering-concurrentiebeding)).
