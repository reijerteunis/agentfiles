# Co-founder fit

Co-founder conflict is one of the most common reasons early startups fail. Have the hard conversations before the paperwork, not after the first crisis.

## 1. Who you need

From `founder-readiness` (the skills gap) and the idea: which role is missing, such as product, engineering, sales or domain expertise. Note the must-haves (skills, time commitment, location) and the values that are non-negotiable.

## 2. Find

Look in the founder's network first: former colleagues are the best signal, because you've seen them work. Then founder communities, accelerator co-founder matching, and events. Draft the outreach as drafts only. Respect the founder's discretion rules while they're employed.

## 3. The structured conversations

Go through these topics over two or three sessions, writing down each person's answer, not a merged one:

1. **Vision:** what company in five years? Lifestyle or venture scale? What would make each of them walk away?
2. **Roles and decisions:** who owns what, who has the final say where, and how deadlocks get broken.
3. **Commitment:** hours, start date, and whether both can live without salary, and for how long.
4. **Money:** how much each person invests, salaries later, and spending decisions.
5. **Equity:** the split, and why. Base it on future contribution, not on whose idea it was. **Vesting** for everyone, typically 4 years with a 1-year cliff, plus good and bad leaver terms.
6. **Conflict:** how disagreements were resolved in past work, and what happens when one person wants out.
7. **Life:** family, health and finances that affect the commitment. Share only what each is comfortable sharing.

Note the mismatches honestly. An early mismatch is cheap; a mismatch after incorporation isn't.

## 4. Test before committing

A paid or time-boxed trial project of 2–6 weeks on the actual idea, with clear deliverables. It's the closest you can get to seeing how you'll work together.

## Output

`cofounder-<name>.md` in the idea folder: the profile needed, notes from each conversation with the mismatches flagged, the trial project plan, and a **term sheet of topics** (roles, split, vesting, leaver terms, IP assignment to the company, decision rules) for the founders' agreement. The agreement itself is drafted by a lawyer.
