# Interview prep

Walk in knowing the company, the people, the three stories to land, and the five questions to ask. Prep is specific to this stage of this interview at this company.

## Inputs

The application folder (`jd.md`, `fit.md`, `research.md`, `application.md`), `profile/stories.md` and `profile/master-cv.md`. Ask for the stage, the interviewers' names and roles, the format and the duration if they aren't in the log. Research each interviewer's public background.

## The prep sheet

Write `interview-prep-<stage>.md`:

1. **The 60-second pitch,** tailored: who you are, the angle, and why this role now. It must sound spoken, not written.
2. **What this stage tests.** A recruiter screen tests fit, logistics and money. A hiring manager tests depth and judgment. A panel tests consistency and collaboration. The CEO or board tests strategy, trust and ambition.
3. **Likely questions,** 10–15 for this stage, each with the best story from `stories.md` (by tag) and 2–3 points to land. Leadership roles add:
   - technology strategy and build versus buy
   - organisation design and the hiring bar
   - the first-90-days plan
   - budget and vendors
   - incidents and accountability
   - AI adoption and governance
   - managing up and across
4. **The hard questions,** answered honestly: gaps from `fit.md`, a failure story, "why are you leaving?" (forward-looking and never critical of the current employer; follow the discretion rules), and salary (the range strategy from preferences).
5. **Questions to ask,** 5–7, built on the research and aimed at the risks. Never ask anything the website answers.
6. **First 90 days,** for leadership roles: listen, decide, build, as one page of hypotheses to test in the conversation, not a plan to impose.
7. **Logistics:** time zone, link or address, who's attending, what to have open, and when to stop prepping.

If a question needs a story that's still TODO in `stories.md`, ask the person to tell it now, and add it there.

## Mock interview

When asked to practise: ask one question at a time, in the interviewer's style. Wait for the answer, then give brief feedback on structure, specificity, length and whether the point landed, and offer a stronger version that uses only their facts.

## Debrief

After the interview, capture what they asked, what landed, what didn't, new information about the role, and the next steps. Append it to the application log. Draft a thank-you note with the `outreach-message` skill.
