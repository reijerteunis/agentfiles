# Job description writer

A job description is the first product a candidate sees from a company. The best ones read like a clear brief from a future manager: what you'll achieve, with whom, under what constraints, and how to tell if it's for you.

## Start from the outcomes

Before writing a word, get these from the hiring manager. Ask if they're missing:

1. **Why this role exists now:** growth, a backfill, a new initiative.
2. **Outcomes after 6 and 12 months,** stated as results, not activities.
3. **Must-haves** (at most five), each something you'd reject a candidate for lacking. Everything else is a nice-to-have.
4. Team, reporting line, stack, stage and the hard problems.
5. Pay range, location and remote policy, and the interview process.

## Structure

1. **The role in two sentences:** the mission and why it matters now.
2. **What you'll achieve:** the 6- and 12-month outcomes.
3. **What you'll work on:** the real problems, the team and the stack.
4. **You'll thrive here if:** the must-haves, written as evidence ("you've led a team through a platform migration"), not as years of experience.
5. **Nice to have:** short, and clearly optional.
6. **What we offer:** the pay range, equity and benefits, location and remote policy, and growth.
7. **How we hire:** the stages, the timeline, and who you'll meet.

400 to 700 words. Then write a LinkedIn version: 5 lines with the key facts and the range.

## Rules

- **Pay transparency.** Include the range. The EU Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970) gives applicants the right to the starting pay or its range before the interview and bans questions about pay history, once national law applies it. The EU deadline was 7 June 2026; the Netherlands plans 1 January 2027, with the bill still in parliament in October 2026. Write postings to that standard now.
- **Inclusive language.** No "rockstar", "ninja", "young and dynamic" or "digital native", no gendered wording, no requirements that proxy for age or nationality. Don't use years of experience as a must-have; use evidence of capability instead.
- **No laundry lists.** Twelve technologies as must-haves tells candidates the team doesn't know what it needs.
- **Honest about the hard parts:** the legacy system, the on-call duty, the travel. That filters better than polish.

## Check before delivering

Read it as the ideal candidate: would they recognise themselves in under 30 seconds? Then read it as someone you shouldn't hire: does anything stop them applying? Fix what fails.
