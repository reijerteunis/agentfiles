# Cover letter

Most cover letters are skipped because they could have been sent to anyone. Write one that's obviously about this job, this company and this person, and short enough to read in a minute.

## Before writing

Read the application's `jd.md`, `fit.md` (especially **the angle**) and `research.md` (especially **hooks**), plus `profile/master-cv.md`. If the person has a voice or context skill, load it and follow its voice rules. Their voice beats any default here.

## Structure, 250 to 350 words

1. **Opening, 2–3 sentences.** A specific, true connection: something the company did, said or is clearly working on, and why it matters to this person. Never start with "I am writing to apply".
2. **Proof, one paragraph.** Use the angle plus one more result that maps to a must-have. Give concrete numbers from the master CV, explained in the language of the posting.
3. **Why them, 2–3 sentences.** What specifically draws the person to this company now, based on the research. No praise you couldn't say to their face.
4. **Close, 1–2 sentences.** Say what you'd bring in the first months, then a plain, confident ask for a conversation.

## Rules

- Every fact comes from the master CV. At the end of the draft, add an HTML comment that lists the IDs you used, for example `<!-- evidence: bc-2, bc-6 -->`. Remove it before sending.
- **Banned:** "I am writing to express my interest", "passionate", "I believe I would be a great fit", "dynamic", "fast-paced", "leverage", "synergy", and rhetorical triads. No em or en dashes unless the person's voice allows them.
- Address a named person when the research found one. Otherwise use "Dear <Company> team".
- Match the posting's language. If it's written in Dutch, write in Dutch.

## Output

Write `cover-letter.md`: the letter ready to paste, then the evidence comment. In the reply, give the opening line and the word count, and ask whether to adjust the tone.
