# Channel plan

Early companies spread themselves over ten channels and learn nothing. Pick the one or two where the ICP already pays attention and where the person has an edge, test them properly with targets set in advance, and double down on what works. The ring method follows *Traction* by Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares.

## 1. List the candidates

Go through the full list for this ICP and motion: the warm network and referrals, outbound (email, calls, social), content and SEO, being cited by AI assistants (generative engine optimisation), communities and forums, events and talks, partners and integrations (marketplaces, app stores), PR, paid search, paid social, sponsorships, newsletters, affiliates, resellers, product loops (invites, sharing, free tools), and free tools as marketing (calculators, scorecards, templates).

## 2. Score them

For each candidate, note:

- **Where the ICP is:** evidence from interviews of where they look for solutions.
- **Expected cost per customer:** a labelled guess at first.
- **Time to the first signal.**
- **The person's edge:** an audience, a network, a skill or content that already exists.
- **Fit with the price:** outbound can't pay for itself on a €20 a month product; ads rarely carry a €50,000 deal.
- **The discretion rules:** some channels may be closed.

## 3. Pick the rings

- **Inner ring:** one or two channels to test properly now.
- **Middle ring:** two or three cheap tries for when the inner ring fails.
- **Parked:** everything else, each with the reason.

## 4. Targets before tests

For each inner-ring channel, write down before starting:

- the experiment: what, how much, for how long
- the leading indicator: replies, sign-ups or booked calls
- the target that means "double down" and the floor that means "stop"
- the budget in money and hours

Hand complex tests to the `experiment-design` skill if venture-lab is installed.

## 5. Check the unit economics

Set the maximum cost per customer for each channel from the customer's value and the gross margin. As a rule of thumb, CAC payback under 12 months for small businesses and under 18–24 months for mid-market; consumer subscriptions need much less. A channel that works but can't stay under that ceiling won't scale.

## Gotchas

- Channels saturate. What worked for someone else last year may not work now: test, don't copy.
- Early attribution is mostly asking: add "how did you hear about us?" to sign-up forms and calls.
- Ads before the positioning and the page work waste money. Fix conversion first.
- Communities punish promotion. Contribute first, and follow each community's rules.

## Output

`strategy/channels.md`: the scored list, the rings, the experiments with targets and floors, the budget and the review date. The weekly review measures each channel against its target.
