# Positioning

Positioning is the context you set so the right customers understand what the product is, why it's better for them, and why it matters now. Most positioning fails because it starts with the product. Start with what customers would do if the product didn't exist. The order below follows April Dunford's method from *Obviously Awesome*.

## Inputs

`strategy/icp.md`, the proof bank, interview and call notes, the market map if venture-lab made one, and the competitors' public pages.

## Steps

1. **Competitive alternatives:** what the best-fit customers would use if you didn't exist: a competitor, a spreadsheet, an agency, an intern, or doing nothing. Use what customers say, not an analyst's list.
2. **Unique attributes:** what you have or do that the alternatives don't: capabilities, approach, expertise, business model, data. Only what's true today, each with proof.
3. **Value:** for each attribute, the outcome it enables for the customer, as measurable as the evidence allows. Group the outcomes into two or three value themes.
4. **Best-fit customers:** who cares most about that value, and the characteristics that make them care. This usually sharpens the ICP.
5. **Market category:** the frame that makes the value obvious.
   - An **existing category** when you can win against the known players on terms buyers already use.
   - A **subcategory** ("X for Y") when you win a specific segment of an existing category.
   - A **new category** only when the first two would hide the value, and you can afford to educate a market.
6. **Relevant trend** (optional): a shift customers already believe in that makes the value more urgent. Never a bandwagon.
7. **Write it down:** a positioning canvas with the six components, a one-paragraph positioning statement for internal use, and the sales narrative it implies: the problem with the alternatives, what changed, the new approach, the value, the proof.

## Test it

- **Five-second test:** show a draft hero line to five people from the ICP. Can they say what it is, who it's for and why it's different?
- **In calls:** lead with the narrative and note where buyers lean in and where they get confused.
- It works when best-fit customers say "that's exactly my problem" and poor fits rule themselves out. Record the results in `positioning.md`.

## Gotchas

- A unique attribute without proof is a claim, not positioning. Add proof to the bank or drop the attribute.
- "Better, faster, cheaper than everyone" is no position. Say who you're better for.
- Don't ridicule the status quo. Customers chose it for reasons; name those reasons.
- Comparisons with named competitors must be accurate, verifiable and fair (EU comparative advertising rules). Keep the evidence.

## Output

`strategy/positioning.md`: the canvas, the statement, the narrative, the test plan and its results, and the changes it implies for `icp.md`. Then hand over to `messaging`.
