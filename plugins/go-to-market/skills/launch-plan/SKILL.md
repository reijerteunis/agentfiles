# Launch plan

A launch is a burst of attention you can plan. Size it to what's at stake, finish the assets before the date, make launch day a checklist, and judge the result against a goal set beforehand.

## 1. Tier

| Tier | What | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A new product, a new market, a major release | Every audience, press or communities, six weeks of preparation |
| 2 | A meaningful feature or a new plan | Customers, site, email and social; two to three weeks |
| 3 | Improvements and fixes | Changelog and an in-app note; days |

## 2. Goal and metrics

One goal, for example "40 qualified sign-ups and 10 booked calls within 14 days", plus the leading metrics and how they're measured (UTM tags per channel, a "how did you hear about us?" question). Write them down before launch.

## 3. Audiences and messages

Existing customers first, and personally for the important ones; then the waitlist and newsletter, the ICP who don't know you yet, communities, partners and press. One message per audience, from `messaging`.

## 4. Assets checklist

Each asset gets an owner and a due date:

- the landing page or the updated page (`landing-page`)
- the announcement: a blog post or changelog entry
- emails: a sequence for the list (`nurture-sequence`) and personal notes to key customers
- social posts, a short demo video or GIF, and screenshots
- an FAQ with objection answers, docs and onboarding
- a sales one-pager, and a briefing for anyone who answers questions
- tracking, tested end to end

## 5. Timeline

- **T−6 to T−4 weeks:** the goal, the tier, a positioning check, the asset list with owners and dates. Line up early users for feedback and testimonials, with permission.
- **T−3 to T−1 weeks:** assets drafted and reviewed, tracking tested, beta users primed, community posts drafted to each community's rules.
- **T−1 day:** freeze. Check the links, sign-up, payment, emails and load.
- **Launch day:** the runbook. Posting order by the hour, who answers comments and support, a status check every few hours, and a plan to pause or roll back if something breaks.
- **T+1 to T+14 days:** follow up with every sign-up and lead, publish a follow-on piece, share early results.
- **T+4 weeks:** the review.

## 6. Launch platforms

- **Product Hunt:** prepare the page, the maker's first comment and the visuals. Tell your community the launch is live, but don't ask for upvotes; the platform penalises it.
- **Hacker News:** "Show HN" only for something people can try now. Write plainly, answer in the comments, and never ask anyone for votes or comments.
- **Communities and newsletters:** follow each one's rules on self-promotion. Contribute; don't broadcast.

## 7. Review

Against the goal: what each channel delivered, conversion through the funnel, what broke, and what to keep for the next launch. Record it in the launch file.

## Gotchas

- Launch to existing customers and your list first: they convert best and spread the word.
- One launch rarely makes a company; the follow-up weeks bring most of the results. Plan them.
- Avoid dates that clash with holidays in your main markets or big industry events, unless you're at the event.
- Press embargoes and exclusives are promises. Keep them.

## Output

`launches/YYYY-MM-<slug>.md`: the tier, goal and metrics, audiences, the asset list with owners and dates, the timeline, the launch-day runbook and, after four weeks, the review. Every public post and email is a draft the person publishes.
