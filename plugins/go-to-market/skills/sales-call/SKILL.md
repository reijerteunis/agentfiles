# Sales call

A sales call is the buyer's meeting about their problem. Learn enough to know whether you can help, show only what matters to them, and leave with a concrete next step in both calendars.

## Prepare

1. Read the deal file and the account brief (or write a short brief; the `outbound-sequence` skill describes one), the positioning, the persona card, the pricing policy and the proof bank.
2. **Hypotheses:** the two or three pains this account most likely has, and the evidence for each.
3. **Agenda** to propose in the first minute: their goals for the call, your questions, a short relevant demo or examples if useful, and next steps.
4. **Discovery questions (SPICED):**
   - **Situation:** how they do it today, with which tools and people.
   - **Pain:** what isn't working, in their words, with examples.
   - **Impact:** what it costs them in time, money, risk or growth, quantified by them.
   - **Critical event:** why now, and the date something has to be true by.
   - **Decision:** who decides, how and on what criteria, the process (security, legal, procurement) and the budget.

   For complex enterprise deals, also cover MEDDICC's metrics, champion and competition.
5. **Likely objections,** with honest answers and proof.
6. **The next step to aim for:** a session with their team, a pilot proposal, a meeting with the decision maker. Specific, with a date.

## Demo, when it fits

Show, don't tour. For each pain they confirmed: say what they'll see and why it matters to them, show it in their context (their data or something close to it), then connect it to the impact they described. Three pains at most, and check in after each. Never show unreleased work as if it were available.

## During the call

Note their words for the pain and the impact, the numbers they gave, the critical event and its date, the people and the process, the objections and how they landed, and the exact next step agreed.

## Debrief

1. **Update the deal file:** stage, value if known, close date, `next_step`, `next_step_due`, `updated`, a SPICED summary and a log entry.
2. **Qualify honestly.** With no pain, no impact or no critical event, say so and suggest parking or disqualifying rather than chasing.
3. **Draft the follow-up email:** thanks, their pain and impact in their words, what was agreed, the next step and its date, and anything promised. A draft only.
4. **Feed what you learned back:** new objections and their best answers go into `strategy/messaging.md`, and new proof goes into the proof bank with permission.

## Gotchas

- In discovery, talk less than half the time.
- Don't quote prices outside the policy on the call. Say you'll come back with options.
- Recording a call needs everyone's consent; ask at the start. Notes are personal data: keep them factual and about the business.
- Never promise roadmap items, dates or integrations you can't commit to, and write down what you did say.

## Output

Before the call, a one-page prep sheet: hypotheses, agenda, questions, objections and the next step to aim for. After the call, the updated deal file and the follow-up draft.
