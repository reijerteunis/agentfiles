# Outreach rules

**As of October 2026. Not legal advice.** The rules depend on the recipient's country, not the sender's, and they change. Verify them before a campaign, and update this file and its date when you do.

## The EU baseline

- **ePrivacy (Directive 2002/58/EC, Article 13):** unsolicited commercial email to natural persons needs prior consent. The exception is existing customers whose details you got in a sale, for your own similar products, with an opt-out offered when the details were collected and in every message. Each member state decides how far the rule also protects companies and work addresses.
- **GDPR** applies to any data about an identifiable person, including a work address like jo@company.com:
  - a lawful basis, for B2B prospecting usually legitimate interest, with a written balancing test
  - transparency: tell people where you got their details, at the latest in the first message
  - data minimisation, and deletion when the data is no longer needed
  - an unconditional right to object to direct marketing: stop at once, and keep them on a suppression list
- **Social platforms:** messages may count as electronic mail under these rules in some countries, so treat them with the same care. Follow each platform's terms; LinkedIn forbids automation and scraping.

## By recipient country

| Country | Cold email to businesses | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Netherlands | Consent needed for email to named people, including work addresses like name@company.nl (Telecommunicatiewet art. 11.7, enforced by the ACM). Generic addresses like info@ may fall outside the rule, but the ACM hasn't confirmed this. | Keep proof of consent for five years after sending. The existing-customer exception applies. |
| Germany | Prior express consent for email advertising to any recipient, businesses included (UWG § 7). | Cold calls to businesses need at least presumed consent. |
| United Kingdom | Allowed to corporate subscribers (companies, LLPs) without consent, if you identify yourself and offer an opt-out (PECR). Sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals and need consent. | UK GDPR applies to named contacts. |
| United States | Allowed without consent (CAN-SPAM): accurate headers, an honest subject line, a clear statement that it's an advertisement, a physical postal address, and an opt-out honoured within 10 business days. | State laws differ for texts and calls. |
| Other EU countries | Check the national ePrivacy law. Many require consent for email to named people at businesses. | |

## Defaults that work everywhere

- Where cold email needs consent: warm introductions, referrals, events, calls where allowed, social messages written and sent by hand, content that brings people to you, or a first contact through an allowed channel that asks permission to email.
- Every email: the sender's real identity, a working opt-out and the company's address.
- Per contact, record the source, the date, the basis (consent, existing customer or legitimate interest) and any opt-out.
